# Blog Editorial Guidelines

This document establishes the quality standards and editorial principles for blog posts generated from RUNS.md entries.

## Purpose

The purpose of these guidelines is to ensure that all generated blog posts are:
- Meaningful and valuable to readers
- Well-structured and easy to read
- Accurate and truthful
- Consistent in tone and style

## Content Value Principles

### 1. Only Publish Meaningful Content

Blog posts should only be generated for runs that contain substantive information. Exclude runs that:
- Are generic error messages like "api would not answer" without context
- Contain only trivial information like "Turns: 12"
- Are completely blank or empty
- Are administrative notes without technical substance

### 2. Focus on Learnings and Insights

Each blog post should communicate:
- What was attempted
- What was learned
- What problems were encountered and solved
- How the experience contributed to growth

### 3. Avoid Repetitive Content

Do not generate posts for runs that:
- Are duplicates of previous runs (same outcome, same note, same day)
- Contain identical or nearly identical notes
- Add no new information beyond what was already documented

## Structural Guidelines

### 1. Clear Title

Every post must have a clear, descriptive title that:
- Reflects the actual content of the post
- Uses the format: "Action-oriented phrase" or "Problem + Solution"
- Is not just "Run X: api_error"
- Includes context when appropriate

### 2. Logical Flow

Posts should follow this structure:
- **Context**: What was being worked on
- **The Attempt**: What was tried or implemented
- **The Result**: What happened and why
- **The Lesson**: What was learned and how to apply it

### 3. Concise and Focused

- Keep paragraphs short (2-4 sentences)
- Use bullet points for lists
- Avoid unnecessary technical jargon
- Focus on the most important information

## Tone and Style

### 1. Professional but Accessible

- Write for a general technical audience
- Explain technical concepts when needed
- Avoid overly academic language
- Use examples to illustrate points

### 2. Honest and Transparent

- Admit when things went wrong
- Explain failures and what was learned
- Be truthful about limitations and constraints
- Don't sugarcoat problems

### 3. Constructive

- Focus on what can be learned, not just what went wrong
- Provide actionable insights
- Suggest improvements for future attempts
- Frame failures as learning opportunities

## Quality Standards

### 1. Readability

- Use proper headings (H1, H2, H3)
- Include subheadings for longer posts
- Use bold text for emphasis
- Format code snippets properly

### 2. Accuracy

- Double-check technical details
- Verify dates and numbers
- Ensure code examples work
- Cite sources when appropriate

### 3. Formatting

- Use consistent Markdown syntax
- Follow Jekyll frontmatter conventions
- Use proper table formatting
- Include relevant links

## Review Process

### 1. Pre-Generation Checks

Before generating a post:
- Review the run note for substantive content
- Determine if the content is unique and valuable
- Identify the key learning or insight
- Plan the structure and flow

### 2. Post-Generation Review

After generation:
- Check that the title is clear and accurate
- Verify the content follows the structure guidelines
- Ensure tone is professional and accessible
- Confirm all technical details are accurate
- Check that formatting is correct

### 3. Approval Criteria

A post should only be published if it:
- Meets all content value principles
- Follows structural guidelines
- Maintains appropriate tone and style
- Passes all quality standards
- Is reviewed and approved by human editor

## Special Cases

### 1. Tool Fixes and Discoveries

Posts about tool fixes should:
- Explain what was broken and why
- Detail the fix process
- Describe the verification steps
- Note any side effects or trade-offs

### 2. Platform Insights

Posts about platform discoveries should:
- Describe the discovery
- Explain why it matters
- Show how to use the new knowledge
- Include examples and best practices

### 3. Failures and Mistakes

Posts about failures should:
- Analyze what went wrong
- Identify root causes
- Explain lessons learned
- Provide actionable takeaways

## Continuous Improvement

Editorial guidelines should be reviewed and updated:
- After publishing 10-20 blog posts
- When new patterns emerge in generated content
- When reader feedback indicates issues
- When technical or structural changes occur

These guidelines ensure that the automated blog generation system produces content that is genuinely valuable, well-written, and aligned with the repository's educational mission.
